Looking for an Anytune alternative? If you're on Windows or Linux, where Anytune doesn't run, or you'd like a player that's free all the way through, I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. If you practise with a phone or a tablet on your music stand, EarBee isn't it: it runs on computers only, and I'll be fair about what else Anytune does better.

Below: what Anytune is, where it runs, how the two compare, and which one I'd pick for what.

## What is Anytune?

[Anytune](https://anytune.app/) is a practice player from Anytune, Inc., for musicians who learn from recordings. In its own words, it slows "down music without changing the pitch", and it can "adjust the pitch of your music to your instrument or your voice". Around that it has:

- **Markers and loops**, to repeat the passage you're working on.
- **An EQ**, which it calls FineTouch Parametric EQ.
- **Isolating an instrument.** On iPhone, iPad and Mac, this is [ReFrame](https://anytune.app/reframe), which lets you "view your sound stage and isolate an instrument like cropping a photo". It works from where each instrument sits between the left and right speakers, using "pan position and EQ", and Anytune says plainly that "the separation is not perfect". On Android there's a different feature, Demix, for the same job.
- **For playing on stage:** lyrics or tabs "that scroll with the music", and control "hands-free with MIDI or Bluetooth Keyboard".
- **Getting songs in:** from Dropbox or over Wi-Fi on an iPhone or iPad, the sound from video files, and on Android, Spotify streams.

One thing to know before you choose: "Anytune features vary on Android, Mac Desktop, and iOS versions." So check that the feature you want is in the version for your device.

## Does Anytune run on Windows or Linux?

Not as far as its site says. Anytune offers versions for iPhone and iPad, for Mac (from the Mac App Store, or as a free trial download) and for Android. When I read [its site](https://anytune.app/) in November 2026, it didn't mention Windows or Linux anywhere.

So if you're on a Windows PC or a Linux computer, you need something else. EarBee runs on both, and on Macs too.

## Is Anytune free?

Partly. On the iPhone and iPad it's free to start with, and you "purchase the tools you need as you need them": its ReFrame page, for instance, says ReFrame comes with the Studio Pack, an in-app purchase, or with the premium version, Anytune Pro+. The Mac version is bought, with a free trial to try it first. For today's prices, see [its own site](https://anytune.app/) and its App Store pages.

EarBee is free, all of it: there's [no trial, no paid version and no account](/#free).

## Two ways to isolate an instrument

This is where the two differ most, so it's worth a closer look.

Anytune's ReFrame works from the stereo picture. In many recordings, the instruments are spread out between left and right: the guitar a little to one side, the keys to the other, the voice and the bass in the middle. ReFrame lets you pick out one place in that picture and hear what's there. When the part you want sits on its own, off to one side, that works well, and it's quick. When it shares the middle with the voice and the bass, or the recording is old and in mono, there's little to pick out.

EarBee splits the song into stems instead: vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest, each one in a mixer, where you solo, mute or turn it down. It does this with an AI model that recognises the instruments by their sound, not by where they sit, so it works in the middle of the mix and in mono too. It runs on your computer, so the song isn't uploaded anywhere, and you wait while your computer does the work. You can separate the whole song, or only the loop you're working on.

Neither is perfect. A model is guessing which sound belongs to which instrument, so a stem can carry traces of its neighbours. For working out a part, that's usually more than clean enough. There's more on using stems this way in [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).

## What EarBee adds

### Video, and YouTube

EarBee opens video files with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound. Anytune's site mentions taking the sound from a video, not playing the video itself.

### You can see where you are

EarBee shows the song as a waveform, and you loop a passage by dragging across it. When you've got it, you move the loop on instead of setting it again from scratch.

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in, and others double it, halve it or move it on to the next bar.

### Your work kept

You can mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them. A project file keeps your marks, your loop, the speed, the EQ and the stems, so tomorrow you start where you stopped.

### The same on every computer

EarBee is the same program on Windows, Mac and Linux, with the same features on all three. If you practise on a Windows PC at home and a Mac at work, you don't have to learn it twice.

## What Anytune does better

To be fair to it, there are good reasons to use it:

- **Phones and tablets.** If you practise with an iPad on your music stand, or your phone in your pocket, Anytune is there. EarBee runs on computers only.
- **On stage.** Lyrics and tabs that scroll with the music, and hands-free control from a MIDI device or a Bluetooth keyboard. EarBee has neither.
- **Streams on Android.** Anytune can slow down Spotify streams there; EarBee needs the song as a file or a YouTube link.
- **Quick isolation, when the mix allows it.** ReFrame needs no waiting: when the part you want sits off to one side, you pick it out at once.

## Is there an Anytune alternative for Windows?

Yes. EarBee is a program you install on a Windows PC, and it's free there, as it is on Mac and Linux. It's made for the same job as Anytune: slowing down songs, looping the hard parts and isolating the instrument you're learning.

## Is there an Anytune alternative for Mac?

Yes, EarBee runs on Macs too. If you're on a Mac, you can also simply try both: Anytune for Mac has a free trial, and EarBee is free.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You practise on an iPhone, an iPad or an Android phone:** Anytune.
- **You play on stage, with lyrics or tabs in front of you, and want to control the player hands-free:** Anytune.
- **You're on Windows or Linux:** EarBee.
- **You want to see the player's hands on video, at any speed:** EarBee.
- **You want the instrument out of the middle of the mix, not only from the sides:** EarBee's stems.
- **You're on a Mac and not sure:** try both on the same song for a week, and you'll know.
