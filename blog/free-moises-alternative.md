Looking for a Moises alternative? It depends on what you use it for. If you use it to practise with recordings, to split a song into stems, slow it down and loop a passage, I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. If you want the chords and the lyrics worked out for you, or an app on your phone, EarBee isn't it, and I'll be fair about that too.

Below: what Moises is, what its free plan limits, how the two compare, and which one I'd pick for what.

## What is Moises?

[Moises](https://moises.ai/) is an app from Moises Systems that calls itself "the creative suite for musicians". You give it a song, as a file or a link, and it takes the song apart for you:

- **Stems.** It [separates the recording](https://moises.ai/features/stem-separation/) into its instruments, such as vocals, drums, bass, guitar and keys, so you can solo or mute each one.
- **Chords, key and tempo.** It [shows the chords](https://moises.ai/features/chord-finder/) as the song plays, names [the key](https://moises.ai/features/key-detector/), and has a [metronome that follows the song's tempo](https://moises.ai/features/metronome-online/).
- **Sections and lyrics.** It [finds the song's parts](https://moises.ai/features/song-parts/) and can [write out the lyrics](https://moises.ai/features/ai-audio-transcription/).
- **Speed and pitch.** You can [slow the song down](https://moises.ai/features/audio-speed-changer/) or [change its key](https://moises.ai/features/pitch-changer-shifter/).

Around that it has tools for making music, not only practising it: mastering, voice models, a plugin for recording programs and a studio that runs in the browser.

It runs as a web app in your browser, and as apps for desktop computers, iPhone, iPad and Android.

## Is Moises free?

Partly. In its own words, Moises "offers a free tier that provides a limited number of uploads each month, with access to a select set of features". When I read its site in November 2026, the limits it named for free users were these:

- **Five songs a month, of up to five minutes each** ([its lyrics page](https://moises.ai/features/ai-audio-transcription/)).
- **Chords for the first minute of each song** ([its chord page](https://moises.ai/features/chord-finder/)), and the same for the metronome that follows the song.
- **Speed changes within a narrow range** around the song's own tempo ([its speed page](https://moises.ai/features/audio-speed-changer/)).

The rest is in two paid subscriptions, Premium and Pro. The cleanest stems, the finest separation (Moises counts 27 kinds of stem at its highest tier) and the plugin are in Pro. For today's prices, see [its own site](https://moises.ai/).

So the free plan is a fair way to try it. If you practise every day, you'll meet its limits within the week.

## Stems on your own computer

This is where the two are closest. EarBee splits a song into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest, and gives you a mixer to solo, mute or turn down each one. The difference is where the work is done.

In EarBee, it's done on your computer. The song isn't uploaded anywhere, so nobody counts how many you've done this month or how long they are, and it works without an internet connection once the model is downloaded. You can separate the whole song, or only the loop you're working on.

The price of that is time and choice. Your computer does the work instead of a server, so you wait while it does, and an older computer waits longer. And you get those six parts, not the finer ones Moises offers, like the lead and backing vocals or the kick and the snare on their own.

Neither is perfect, by the way. A model is guessing which sound belongs to which instrument, so a stem can carry traces of its neighbours. For working out a part, that's usually more than clean enough. There's more on using stems this way in [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).

## Why EarBee doesn't detect chords

Because it's made for working them out yourself. A chord chart from an app is like the answers at the back of the book: fine for checking your work, or when you need the song by tonight's gig, but you don't learn much from copying them. When you find a chord yourself, your ear gets a little better at finding the next one.

That's my view, and you don't have to share it. If you'd like to try, here's [how to figure out chords by ear](/blog/figure-out-chords-by-ear/). The stems help a lot: solo the bass and you've got the roots.

## What EarBee adds

### No counting

No uploads to run out of, no first minute only, no subscription to remember to cancel. Everything is in the download.

### You can see where you are

EarBee shows the song as a waveform, and you loop a passage by dragging across it. When you've got it, you move the loop on instead of setting it again from scratch.

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in, and others double it, halve it or move it on to the next bar.

### Stems, speed and pitch together

The stems keep in step at any speed or pitch. So you can solo the guitar, slow it to half speed and loop two bars of it, all at once.

### Video, and YouTube

EarBee opens video files with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound.

### Your work kept

You can mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them. A project file keeps your marks, your loop, the speed, the EQ and the stems, so tomorrow you start where you stopped.

## What Moises does better

To be fair to it, there are good reasons to use it, and to pay for it:

- **Phone and tablet apps, and the browser.** If you practise with your phone on a music stand, or on a computer where you can't install anything, Moises is there. EarBee runs on computers only.
- **The chords, the key and the lyrics,** worked out for you. If that's what you came for, EarBee won't replace it.
- **Finer stems.** The lead vocal apart from the backing vocals, the kick apart from the snare, the rhythm guitar apart from the solo.
- **No waiting on your own computer.** The separating is done for you, which matters most on an old laptop or a phone.
- **Tools for making music:** mastering, voice models, recording, and a plugin that brings the stems into a recording program.

## Is there an open-source Moises alternative?

EarBee is one: its code is public, and anyone can read it, change it or build it themselves. It isn't a copy of Moises, though. It does the practising part (stems, speed, pitch, loops, beats, video) and leaves out the detecting and the producing.

## Is there a Moises alternative for PC?

Yes. EarBee is a program you install on a Windows PC, a Mac or a Linux computer, and it's free on all three.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You practise on a phone or tablet:** Moises.
- **You want the chords or the lyrics handed to you:** Moises.
- **You practise at a computer, and want stems, slowing down and loops without a monthly limit:** EarBee.
- **You want to work songs out by ear, and see the player's hands on video:** EarBee.
- **You're not sure:** EarBee costs nothing, and Moises has a free plan. Use both on the same song for a week, and you'll know.
