Looking for a Song Surgeon alternative? If you'd rather not pay for a practice player, or you're on Linux, where Song Surgeon doesn't run, I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. Song Surgeon does some things EarBee doesn't, such as finding the chords and speeding a loop up as you practise, and I'll be fair about those too.

Below: what Song Surgeon is, what it costs, how the two compare, and which one I'd pick for what.

## What is Song Surgeon?

[Song Surgeon](https://www.songsurgeon.com/) is a desktop app from Todd, Michael and James, Inc., first released in 2007. It's made for changing the speed and the key of a recording without distorting it, for practice, transcribing, karaoke or backing tracks. Its current version, Song Surgeon 6, comes in three editions, Basic, Standard and Pro, and [which features you get](https://www.songsurgeon.com/features.html) depends on the edition. Across them, it has:

- **Speed and key changes,** with the key detected for you, and an option on the higher editions that keeps voices from sounding like chipmunks.
- **Loops,** more of them in the higher editions, and markers for information, bars and beats.
- **An EQ** with 31 bands and presets (Standard and Pro).
- **Tempo detection,** in every edition, and chord detection in Standard and Pro. Pro also detects the notes and shows them as the song plays.
- **A speed trainer** (Standard and Pro), which makes the playback a little faster (or slower) each time the loop comes round.
- **Capturing sound from your computer** (Standard and Pro): whatever plays, including streaming services, comes into Song Surgeon to practise with.
- **In Pro:** recording, a metronome that follows the song's tempo, and some audio editing.

It opens audio files, and the sound from video files (not the picture). You can save your changes as a new audio file.

## Does Song Surgeon run on Linux?

Not as far as its site says. It's a desktop app for Windows and Mac. There's also a free mobile app, Song Surgeon Trainer, for phones, but it isn't the full program. When I read [its site](https://www.songsurgeon.com/) in November 2026, it didn't mention Linux anywhere.

So on a Linux computer, you need something else. EarBee runs there, and on Windows and Macs too. If you're on Linux, here's [what else there is for transcribing music on Linux](/blog/music-transcription-software-linux/).

## How much does Song Surgeon cost?

It's a one-time purchase: Song Surgeon says it has no recurring fee, and you keep what you buy. The three editions have different prices, and Pro has extra modules you can buy on top, such as automatic beat detection and notation export. Separating the instruments and the voice, which it calls Vocal Removal Instrument Isolation, is a Pro add-on too, paid for by the month or by the year. For today's prices, see [its own site](https://www.songsurgeon.com/).

Before you buy, there's a free demo with four hours of use, and its site offers a 60-day money-back guarantee.

EarBee is free, all of it: there's [no trial, no paid version and no account](/#free).

## What EarBee adds

### Everything, in one free download

With Song Surgeon, what you get depends on which edition you buy, and on the add-ons. EarBee has one version, and it's free: the stems, the beats and bars, the EQ, the project files and the video are all in it.

### Video, and YouTube

EarBee opens video files with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound. Song Surgeon takes the sound from a video, without the picture.

### Stems, split on your computer

EarBee splits a song into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest, each one in a mixer, where you solo, mute or turn it down. An AI model does it on your computer, so nothing is uploaded, and you can split the whole song or only the loop you're working on. There's more on using stems in [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in, and others double it, halve it or move it on to the next bar. You can also mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them.

### Linux, the same as Windows and Mac

EarBee is the same program on all three, with the same features. If you use Linux at home and Windows at work, you don't have to learn it twice.

## What Song Surgeon does better

To be fair to it, there are good reasons to use it:

- **The speed trainer.** Song Surgeon can play a loop a little faster each time it comes round, so you work up to full speed without stopping to change it. In EarBee you change the speed yourself.
- **Chords and notes detected.** If you want them found for you and shown as the song plays, Song Surgeon does that, in its higher editions. EarBee doesn't, on purpose: it's made for working them out by ear (I wrote about why in [the post about Capo](/blog/capo-for-windows/), which does the same).
- **Saving the changed audio.** You can save a slowed-down or transposed version as a file, to play anywhere. EarBee changes the sound as it plays, but doesn't save it.
- **Capturing and recording.** Song Surgeon can take in the sound of anything playing on your computer, and in Pro, record. EarBee needs the song as a file or a YouTube link, and doesn't record.
- **Paid once.** If you'd rather pay for a tool with support behind it than use a free one, Song Surgeon is a one-time purchase, not a subscription (apart from the isolation add-on).

## Which one would I pick?

- **You're on Linux:** EarBee. Song Surgeon doesn't run there.
- **You want a loop that speeds up by itself as you practise:** Song Surgeon.
- **You want the chords found for you:** Song Surgeon.
- **You want to save slowed-down versions to play on other devices:** Song Surgeon.
- **You want to see the player's hands on video, at any speed:** EarBee.
- **You want the instrument out of the mix without paying for an add-on:** EarBee's stems.
- **You're on Windows or a Mac and not sure:** try both on the same song. Song Surgeon has a free demo, and EarBee is free.
