Looking for Capo for Windows? There isn't one: Capo runs on Macs, iPhones and iPads only, and its makers say they have no plans for a Windows version. If you're on a Windows PC or a Linux computer, I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. It doesn't do everything Capo does: it won't show you the chords, and I'll be fair about that too.

Below: what Capo is, where it runs, what it costs, how the two compare, and which one I'd pick for what.

## What is Capo?

[Capo](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/) is an app from SuperMegaUltraGroovy for learning songs from recordings. In its own words, it helps you "learn your favorite songs without tabs or sheet music": it analyses a song "to reveal its beats, chords, and key". Around that it has:

- **Chords, found for you.** It detects them as the song plays, shows the chord diagrams for guitar, bass, ukulele, banjo, mandolin or piano, and lets you correct them.
- **Slowing down and transposing,** without changing the pitch, or changing the key.
- **Loops and sections,** with markers and regions that loop in time with the beat.
- **Isolating a part.** Its iPhone and iPad page says you can "solo or mute any instrument or vocal".
- **On the Mac, writing it down:** a tab view for working out a part note by note, and lead sheets and MIDI to export.
- **Everything on your device.** Its FAQ says "Capo processes everything locally": the song isn't uploaded.

It opens song files (MP3, WAV, M4A and others), but not streams: in its FAQ's words, it "can't access streaming tracks from services like Spotify or Apple Music".

## Is there a Capo for Windows?

No. [Capo's FAQ](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/) answers that question directly: "No, and we have no plans to build Capo for Windows or Android at this time." When I read its site in November 2026, it didn't mention Linux either.

Some download sites list a "Capo for Windows" anyway. Since the developer says there isn't one, I'd stay away from those downloads: at best they're something else, at worst they're not safe.

So on a Windows PC or a Linux computer, you need another program. EarBee runs on both, and on Macs too.

## Is Capo free?

Partly. There's a Free Edition that you can "use for as long as you like", and the rest is in subscriptions: Capo Pro, bought for the Mac or for iPhone and iPad, and Capo Platinum, which covers all your Apple devices. For the Mac there's also a one-time version, sold through music shops. In November 2026, the Free Edition left out, among other things, saving your whole project (your regions, sections and chord edits) and exporting audio. For today's prices and what's in each, see [its pricing page](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/pricing/).

EarBee is free, all of it: there's [no trial, no paid version and no account](/#free).

## Chords found for you, or worked out by ear

This is the biggest difference between the two, so it's worth a closer look.

Capo listens to the song and writes the chords out for you, and you correct what it got wrong. If you want to play along tonight, that's quick: the chords are there, with the diagrams, and you check them as you play.

EarBee doesn't guess the chords. On purpose: it's made for working them out yourself, by ear. It slows the song down, loops the change you're stuck on and lets you bring the bass forward, and you do the listening. That's slower the first time. But a detected chord is a guess, and you check a guess by listening anyway, so the skill you need is the same. Each song you work out yourself makes the next one quicker, with or without an app. If you'd like to try, here's [how to figure out chords by ear](/blog/figure-out-chords-by-ear/), starting from the bass.

I wrote more about this side of it, the automatic guess against working it out yourself, in [the post about AnthemScore](/blog/anthemscore-free-alternative/).

## What EarBee adds

### Windows and Linux, the same as on the Mac

EarBee is the same program on Windows, Mac and Linux, with the same features on all three. If you practise on a Windows PC at home and a Mac somewhere else, you don't have to learn it twice.

### Video, and YouTube

EarBee opens video files with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound. Capo's site talks about songs, not video.

### Stems, split on your computer

EarBee splits a song into vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano and the rest, each one in a mixer, where you solo, mute or turn it down. An AI model does it on your computer, so nothing is uploaded, and you can split the whole song or only the loop you're working on. There's more on using stems in [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).

### Everything, free

Marks for each section, with a name and a colour; a project file that keeps your marks, your loop, the speed, the EQ and the stems; the beats and bars. None of it is behind a subscription.

## What Capo does better

To be fair to it, there are good reasons to use it:

- **The chords, written out.** If you want them on the screen as the song plays, with the fingering for your instrument, Capo does that. EarBee doesn't.
- **iPhone and iPad.** Capo is on them, and your projects follow you between devices. EarBee runs on computers only.
- **Writing it down.** On the Mac, Capo has a tab view for working out a part note by note, and exports lead sheets and MIDI. EarBee has neither.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You're on Windows or Linux:** EarBee. Capo doesn't run there.
- **You want the chords shown to you, with diagrams:** Capo.
- **You practise on an iPhone or an iPad:** Capo.
- **You want to learn to hear the chords yourself:** EarBee.
- **You want to see the player's hands on video, at any speed:** EarBee.
- **You're on a Mac and not sure:** try both on the same song. Capo has a free edition, and EarBee is free.
